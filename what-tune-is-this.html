What tune is this?

Naming Irish traditional tunes by ear, while they are still being played.

Everyone at a session has had the same moment. A set starts, you half-know the second tune, and by the time you have hummed enough of it to ask someone the set is over and the name is gone. JigRipper is built for that moment: it listens through the microphone, recognises the tune from a repertoire of over 22,000 Irish traditional tunes, and puts the name and the sheet music in front of you before the players have finished.

How it works

1. It listens

Tap the listening ring and JigRipper starts analysing the sound in the room. It does not need a clean studio recording — it is built for the real thing: a pub, a kitchen, a practice session, a recording playing across the room.

2. It names the tune

A neural network trained specifically on Irish traditional music matches what it hears against the repertoire and names the tune — reel, jig, slip jig, hornpipe, polka, slide, waltz — usually within a few seconds of the melody starting. Because sessions run tunes together into sets, it keeps identifying as the set moves on, so you get the whole set, not just the first tune.

3. It shows the music, and follows along

Every identified tune comes with notation, engraved by JigRipper's own music-typesetting engine. Turn on the cursor and it tracks the player through the score bar by bar, so you can read along with what is actually being played rather than hunting for your place.

Is it "Shazam for Irish music"?

It is the same idea, aimed at a problem Shazam cannot solve. Commercial music recognition matches a specific recording — the exact take, the exact mix. Traditional music is not like that. The reel four people are playing in a kitchen tonight has never been recorded; it is the same tune as a hundred other versions, in a different key, at a different speed, with different ornaments. JigRipper recognises the tune, not the recording, which is why it works on live playing that exists nowhere else.

Where the tunes come from

Tune names, types, keys and settings are derived from the community database of thesession.org, used under the Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0. Details and the share-alike offer are on the privacy and legal page.

Privacy

Recognition happens on your device. Audio is analysed as it arrives and is never recorded to permanent storage, never uploaded and never shared. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no trackers — JigRipper has no server of its own to send anything to.

Questions

Will it work in a noisy pub?

Usually. A clear, reasonably loud source and a room that is not wall-to-wall chatter help most. The hardest case is re-miked audio — a tune played back through a speaker and picked up again.

Does it work for Scottish, Breton or old-time tunes?

The models were trained on the Irish traditional repertoire, and tunes shared with other traditions are often in it. Tunes outside that repertoire are not.

Can I use it to learn a tune?

Yes — that is much of the point. Identify the tune, read the notation, save it to a set, and come back to it later.
